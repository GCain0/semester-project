# Reflection 

Looking back at this project from start to finish, the biggest thing I realized is how much the small decisions add up when you’re trying to make something actually useful. My P01 technically worked, but it didn’t feel finished. It had all the content, but none of the structure or polish that makes a site enjoyable to use. For P02, I wanted the project to feel cleaner, more organized, and more like a real resource that expecting parents could actually benefit from. That mindset shaped almost everything I did during this final stage.

## Decisions
One of the first decisions I made was switching all the long lists into collapsible sections. In P01, every page required a ton of scrolling, especially on a phone. It wasn’t broken, but it wasn’t pleasant either. I kept thinking about how overwhelmed new parents already are, and how the last thing they need is a cluttered website. Collapsible sections immediately made the pages feel more organized and let people open only what they need.

Another big decision was adding two new pages: the Postpartum Recovery Guide and Baby Care Basics. These weren’t part of my original plan, but once I looked at the project as a whole, it felt incomplete without them. Preparing for childbirth doesn’t stop at packing a hospital bag or buying newborn essentials. There’s a whole recovery period afterward, and there’s the day‑to‑day baby care that hits fast. Adding those pages made the project feel more realistic and helpful.

I also made a conscious decision to keep the design simple. Instead of experimenting with different layouts or colors, I stuck with the same button style, spacing, and structure across every page. It made the whole site feel connected and consistent. Finally, I rewrote all my documentation from scratch. I wanted the README and reflection to actually explain the project instead of just checking boxes.

## What Worked
The collapsible sections were the biggest win. They instantly made the site easier to navigate and gave it a more polished feel. The countdown timer on the homepage also turned out better than I expected. It adds a personal touch and makes the site feel tied to a real moment in time. The navigation buttons worked well too — they’re clean, easy to tap, and consistent across the site.

Adding the two new pages made a huge difference. The project feels complete now, not like a partial idea. Everything connects, and each page serves a clear purpose. Overall, I’m happy with how the site looks and functions. It’s simple, organized, and easy to use.

## What I Would Do Differently
If I started over, I’d spend more time planning the structure before building anything. In P01, I jumped straight into writing content, and later I had to reorganize everything to make it more usable. I’d also experiment more with responsive design. The site works on mobile, but I could fine‑tune spacing, font sizes, and layout to make it feel even smoother.

Another thing I’d do differently is adding more interactive features. Checkboxes, progress tracking, or saving checklist items would make the site even more helpful. I’d also consider adding a footer or a theme toggle to give the site a more finished feel.

## What I Learned
I learned how important it is to think about the user’s experience from the beginning. Just because something “works” doesn’t mean it feels good to use. I also learned how valuable consistency is when building a multi‑page website. Every page needs to feel like part of the same system.

I gained confidence in organizing content, writing clean HTML, and using JavaScript for simple interactive features. I learned how to manage a project through multiple iterations and how much polish matters when you’re trying to make something feel complete. Most of all, I learned that I enjoy building practical tools that help people. This project reminded me that clarity and simplicity often matter more than complexity.
